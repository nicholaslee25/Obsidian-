## Favorite Styles

- **Long shutter speed** — slow shutter captures motion blur while a stationary subject stays sharp. Water, traffic, light trails, stars. Needs a tripod.
- **Reflection shots** — water, glass, mirrors. Symmetry and doubled compositions.

## Settings (plain English)

**ISO** — sensor sensitivity to light.
- Low (100–400): clean image, needs decent light
- High (1600+): brighter in dark, adds grain/noise
- Rule: keep ISO as low as the conditions allow

**Shutter speed** — how long the sensor is exposed.
- Fast (1/500s+): freezes motion
- Slow (1/30s and below): blurs motion — the long exposure effect
- Long exposure needs: tripod + remote shutter or timer

**Aperture (f-stop)** — how wide the lens opening is.
- Low f-number (f/1.8): wide open, blurry background (bokeh), good in low light
- High f-number (f/11+): everything in focus, good for landscapes

## Gear Curiosities

### What's so special about a Hasselblad camera?

Medium format sensor (much larger than full-frame 35mm) = more resolution, better dynamic range, shallower depth of field at the same aperture. Legendary build quality and modular system (interchangeable backs, lenses, viewfinders — you're buying a system, not a body). Historically the camera NASA chose for the Moon landings. Price reflects both the sensor size and the brand's medium-format monopoly at the high end.

### What is the "holy trinity" of lenses?

Three zoom lenses that together cover every common focal length at a constant wide aperture (usually f/2.8): a wide-angle (e.g. 14-24mm), a standard/mid (24-70mm), and a telephoto (70-200mm). "Holy trinity" because between the three, a shooter rarely needs anything else — the professional standard lens kit.

### What makes RAW files so special?

RAW stores the sensor's unprocessed data straight off the chip — no in-camera compression, sharpening, white balance baking, or color processing applied. A JPEG has already thrown away detail to compress and "finish" the image. RAW keeps all of it, so you can correct exposure, white balance, and color in post with far more room before quality degrades. Tradeoff: much larger file size, and it needs processing software before it looks like a normal photo.

### What is IBIS?

**In-Body Image Stabilization** — the camera body physically shifts the sensor itself (on a floating platform) to counteract handshake, rather than relying only on stabilization built into the lens. Works with any lens attached, including old manual glass with no stabilization of its own. Often combined with lens-based stabilization for even better results.

### When does ISO actually kick in — before or after the ADC?

ISO in a modern digital sensor is mostly **applied after the analog signal comes off the sensor but before/during the ADC (analog-to-digital conversion)** — it's an analog gain boost applied to the raw voltage signal, amplifying it before it's digitized. This is why high ISO on a good sensor still looks better than pushing exposure in software after the fact (digital gain, post-ADC): analog gain amplifies signal and noise together at the point where the signal is still cleanest, while pure digital push amplifies noise that's already been baked into the recorded values. Some newer sensors use "dual gain" / dual conversion gain architectures with two selectable analog gain stages for cleaner high-ISO performance.
