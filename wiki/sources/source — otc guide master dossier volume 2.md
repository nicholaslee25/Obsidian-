ChatGPT-compiled evidence dossier expanding the Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide beyond brand rankings into actual clinical evidence, organized by active ingredient. Core rule established here and carried through the whole pharmacy project: a pharmacist-recommendation percentage (e.g. Imodium 81%) is a survey/preference statistic, never a clinical success rate — the two must always be labeled and stored separately.

Covers deep study-level evidence (PMID/DOI, N, design, effect sizes, CIs, p-values, adverse events) for: elderberry/Sambucol, Oscillococcinum, Pelargonium/Umcka, pseudoephedrine, naproxen, zinc lozenges, guaifenesin, acetaminophen (cold symptoms), combination cold products, and OTC misuse/safety literature. Also defines the evidence-grading scale (High/Moderate/Low/Very Low/No product-specific evidence) and the full data-field schema for downstream AI training (brand/generic/ingredient/dose/effect size/adverse events/etc. as separate fields).

Note on reliability: this is AI-generated research output, not independently peer-reviewed by us. Citations look well-formed (correct PMID format, specific stats) but should be spot-checked before being relied on for real medical decisions — flagged explicitly because one citation (PMID 42014704, "PHYTOBRONCH 2026") is unusually recent and worth double-checking.
