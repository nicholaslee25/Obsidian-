Direct continuation of [[source — otc guide master dossier volume 2]] from the same ChatGPT research thread (https://chatgpt.com/share/6a8b45fa-e980-83ea-a1e0-18e267f5a2e6), pasted into chat once the source's own file-generation tool got rate-limited. Expands the research standard significantly: every ingredient entry should now capture mechanism, FDA status, dose, age restrictions, study-level statistics (RR/OR/HR/MD/SMD/CI/p/NNT/NNH), adverse events, contraindications, special-population flags, and a full citation — with an explicit template for future additions (Part XVII).

Covers the categories the first dossier hadn't reached yet: EENT (artificial tears, ear wax removal, sore throat lozenges), Diagnostics (COVID tests, glucose monitors, lancets, thermometers — explicitly flagged as needing sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV metrics instead of drug-efficacy metrics), Vitamins/Supplements (vitamin C, vitamin D, calcium, magnesium, CoQ10, cranberry, fiber, garlic, omega-3, glucosamine/chondroitin, melatonin, multivitamins, prenatal vitamins, iron, probiotics), Gastrointestinal, Pain/Inflammation, Cough/Cold/Allergy ingredients, First Aid, Topicals, Diabetic Healthcare, Oral Care, Pediatrics, Women's Health, and Durable Goods.

Two things worth remembering from this source specifically: the "Important Negative Evidence" table (Part XIV) — docusate, oral phenylephrine, homeopathic cold remedies, infant simethicone, phenazopyridine, etc. all have explicit "doesn't work as well as assumed" writeups worth preserving — and the hard rule that Pharmacy Times recommendation data (Layer A) and clinical evidence (Layer B) must never be merged into one score.

Same reliability caveat as the parent source: AI-generated, not independently verified — treat citations as leads to check, not settled facts.
