## Evidence Summary
Prenatal vitamins are explicitly framed as "not one generic supplement" — evidence is strongest for specific nutrients/outcomes: folic acid, iron, iodine, vitamin D (baseline-dependent), and calcium (dietary-intake/risk-dependent). The source instructs distinguishing preconception folic acid use, pregnancy iron use, and general prenatal multivitamin use as separate evidence bases with separate endpoints — they should not be pooled into one "prenatal vitamins work" claim.

## Pros
- Strong, specific evidence exists for individual component nutrients (folic acid, iron, iodine) even though the combined-product evidence is more heterogeneous

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Evidence strength varies substantially by nutrient and by outcome — a single overall efficacy grade for "prenatal vitamins" as a category would be misleading
- Vitamin D and calcium benefit are baseline/dietary-intake-dependent, not universal

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Preconception (folic acid) vs. pregnancy (iron) vs. general prenatal multivitamin use are separate evidence questions per source and should not be conflated

## Formulation Caveat
Distinguish preconception folic acid vs. pregnancy iron vs. general prenatal multivitamin use — different evidence bases and endpoints apply to each, per the source's explicit instruction.
