# Honey (Pediatric Cough, Ages 1+ Only)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Natural demulcent/cough remedy
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — evidence for improving nighttime cough in children over 1 year; carries a critical, absolute safety restriction for infants under 12 months
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Nighttime cough in children over 1 year of age.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material.

## Evidence Summary
Evidence supports honey for improving nighttime cough (frequency, severity, sleep) versus no-treatment/placebo-like controls, in children over 1 year of age. **Critical safety restriction, stated explicitly and absolutely in source: never give honey to infants under 12 months, due to botulism risk.**

## Pros
- Evidence for improving nighttime cough frequency, severity, and sleep in children over 1 year, compared to no-treatment/placebo-like controls
- Low-cost, widely available

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- **Absolute contraindication in infants under 12 months: botulism risk.** This is not a relative caution — it is a hard age cutoff per source.

## Contraindications / Interactions
- **Infants under 12 months: contraindicated (botulism risk)**

## Special Population Flags
- **Age is the determining safety factor for this ingredient** — evidence and safety are both explicitly age 1+ only; there is no dose adjustment that makes honey appropriate for infants under 12 months

## Formulation Caveat
Not reported in source material — no distinction between honey types/formulations (e.g., raw vs. processed, honey-based cough syrup products) is given.

No specific Pharmacy Times brand percentage for honey-based pediatric cough products is given in either source document — this section is intentionally omitted rather than inventing one.
