# Iron

## Indication
Correction of iron deficiency / iron-deficiency anemia.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material.

## Evidence Summary
Iron supplementation shows strong benefit when deficiency exists, but potential harm when unnecessary. Evidence questions to track separately include hemoglobin response, ferritin, anemia correction, fatigue, pregnancy outcomes, GI adverse effects, constipation, adherence, and oral vs. IV administration. The source gives an explicit corrective framing: do NOT encode "iron gives you energy" as a general claim — the correct framing is "iron supplementation corrects iron deficiency/iron-deficiency anemia; symptomatic benefit expected when deficiency is present."

## Pros
- Strong, well-established benefit when a true iron deficiency or iron-deficiency anemia is present (hemoglobin/ferritin response, anemia correction, symptomatic benefit including fatigue when deficiency is the cause)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Potential harm when supplementation is unnecessary (i.e., in the absence of deficiency) — the source treats this as a real risk, not merely "no benefit"
- GI adverse effects and constipation are common tolerability issues
- Oral vs. IV administration are separate evidence questions with different adherence/tolerability profiles

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the general "harm when unnecessary" caution

## Special Population Flags
- Pregnancy outcomes are specifically listed as a tracked evidence endpoint, separate from general deficiency correction

## Formulation Caveat
Not reported in source material beyond the oral vs. IV administration distinction noted above.
