## Evidence Summary
Garlic has been studied for blood pressure, cholesterol, cardiovascular risk, and immune claims. The evidence shows small effects on surrogate cardiovascular markers, but evidence for major clinical outcomes is much weaker. Per source's explicit framing: "Garlic lowers BP somewhat" does not equal "garlic prevents heart attacks" — that stronger claim would require cardiovascular-outcome trials, which are not described as available in the source.

## Pros
- Some evidence of small effects on surrogate cardiovascular markers (e.g., blood pressure)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Evidence for major clinical outcomes (e.g., heart attack prevention) is much weaker than the surrogate-marker evidence
- Surrogate-marker improvement should not be read as proof of hard clinical-outcome benefit
