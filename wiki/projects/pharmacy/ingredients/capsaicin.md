# Capsaicin

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Topical TRPV1 agonist (counterirritant/neuropathic-pain agent)
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — supports use for some neuropathic pain, with notable adherence limitations
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Diabetic neuropathy pain.

## Mechanism
Activates TRPV1 receptors, causing functional desensitization of nociceptive (pain-sensing) nerve fibers.

## Evidence Summary
Evidence supports topical capsaicin for some neuropathic pain (continuation source). Problems include a burning sensation on application, adherence difficulty (many patients discontinue due to the burning), and variable benefit across patients. High-concentration capsaicin patches are prescription/specialist therapy; OTC creams available in this database are lower concentration than those specialist products.

## Pros
- Evidence supports use for some neuropathic pain, including diabetic neuropathy

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Burning sensation on application is common and can be significant
- Adherence is a real practical limitation — patients may discontinue due to the burning
- Benefit is variable across patients
- OTC creams are lower concentration than prescription/specialist high-concentration capsaicin patches — do not assume OTC-strength evidence matches high-concentration patch evidence

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
High-concentration capsaicin patches are prescription/specialist therapy, distinct from OTC capsaicin creams — evidence for one should not be assumed to transfer to the other given the substantial concentration difference.
