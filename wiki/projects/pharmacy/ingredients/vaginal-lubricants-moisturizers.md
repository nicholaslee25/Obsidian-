# Vaginal Lubricants and Moisturizers (General)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class — two distinct product types)
**Drug Class:** Lubricant (water-based, silicone-based, oil-based) / vaginal moisturizer
**Evidence Grade:** Established for symptomatic comfort — not a disease-treatment category; the lubricant/moisturizer distinction is the key organizing fact
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

## Indication
Lubricants: reducing friction during sexual activity. Moisturizers: baseline vaginal dryness, used regularly (not just around sexual activity).

## Mechanism
Lubricants act mechanically (reducing friction) via water-based, silicone-based, or oil-based formulations. Moisturizers address baseline tissue hydration on a regular-use basis.

## Evidence Summary
**Critical distinction preserved from source: a lubricant is used around sexual activity; a moisturizer is used regularly for baseline vaginal dryness.** These are not interchangeable products despite being adjacent in marketing. The evidence question for lubricants is symptomatic comfort, not disease treatment. A specific and practically important safety note: oil-based lubricant products can damage latex condoms.

## Pros
- Effective for their intended, narrowly defined purpose (reducing friction during sexual activity, or relieving baseline dryness with regular use)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- **Oil-based lubricants can damage latex condoms** — a specific, practical safety point
- Not a disease-treatment category — the evidence question is symptomatic comfort only

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Oil-based lubricants + latex condoms: material damage/failure risk

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Lubricant type (water-based, silicone-based, oil-based) determines condom compatibility and other practical properties — these should not be treated as interchangeable. Lubricants and moisturizers are also a distinct pair of product types (per-use vs. regular-use), not two names for the same thing.
