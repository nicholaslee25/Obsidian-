# Diabetic Foot Care (General)

**Type:** Care Protocol (not a single active ingredient)

**Evidence Grade:** Prevention-focused — no cream compensates for neuropathy, vascular disease, infection, or poor wound care
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23

## Indication
Prevention of diabetic foot complications.

## Evidence Summary
The primary evidence-based principle is prevention: moisturizing dry skin (while avoiding application between the toes when excess moisture is a problem there), checking feet daily, wearing proper footwear, and avoiding self-treatment of ulcers/calluses in high-risk patients. Per source's explicit framing: "A cream cannot compensate for neuropathy, vascular disease, infection, or poor wound care."

## The Prevention Protocol (per source)
- Moisturize dry skin (avoid between-toe application when excess moisture is a problem there)
- Check feet daily
- Wear proper footwear
- Avoid self-treatment of ulcers/calluses in high-risk patients

## Pros
- Simple, well-defined, low-cost daily prevention protocol

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- No topical product substitutes for addressing neuropathy, vascular disease, infection, or poor wound care directly
- Self-treatment of ulcers/calluses in high-risk diabetic patients is specifically discouraged — this requires professional evaluation

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- High-risk diabetic patients (existing neuropathy, vascular disease, or prior ulceration): self-treatment of ulcers/calluses should be avoided

## Formulation Caveat
"Diabetic" foot creams are evaluated on the same prevention principle as general foot moisturizers — no specific diabetic-labeled cream formulation is shown in source to outperform the general moisturizing/prevention protocol.
