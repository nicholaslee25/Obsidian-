# Fiber

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Dietary fiber supplement (types include psyllium, methylcellulose, wheat dextrin, inulin, partially hydrolyzed guar gum)
**Evidence Grade:** Better-supported than many supplements owing to a direct physiological mechanism and measurable outcomes
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

## Indication
Constipation; also studied for LDL cholesterol, glycemic response, and satiety.

## Mechanism
Direct physiological/bulking mechanism affecting stool frequency and consistency (not elaborated further in source material).

## Evidence Summary
Fiber is better-supported than many other supplements because of its direct physiological mechanism and measurable outcomes (stool frequency/consistency, constipation, LDL, glycemic response, satiety). However, fiber type matters — psyllium, methylcellulose, wheat dextrin, inulin, and partially hydrolyzed guar gum are not identical. For constipation specifically, soluble/bulking fibers can help meaningfully; adequate fluid intake and gradual dose escalation matter for tolerability.

## Pros
- Direct physiological mechanism with measurable outcomes across several endpoints (stool frequency/consistency, LDL, glycemic response, satiety)
- Soluble/bulking fibers can meaningfully help constipation

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Fiber type matters significantly — different fiber types (psyllium, methylcellulose, wheat dextrin, inulin, partially hydrolyzed guar gum) are not interchangeable
- Requires adequate fluid intake and gradual dose escalation for tolerability; the source does not specify what happens without these but implies tolerability issues (e.g., bloating/gas) are a practical concern with rapid dose increases

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags

## Formulation Caveat
Fiber type matters — psyllium, methylcellulose, wheat dextrin, inulin, and partially hydrolyzed guar gum are explicitly named in source as non-identical and should be evaluated separately rather than as one interchangeable "fiber" category.
