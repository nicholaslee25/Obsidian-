# Loperamide

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antidiarrheal (peripheral opioid-receptor agonist)
**Evidence Grade:** High for therapeutic-dose symptomatic relief of acute diarrhea
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Symptomatic reduction of stool frequency in acute, noninfectious diarrhea.

## Mechanism
Peripheral μ-opioid receptor agonism in intestinal smooth muscle — decreases intestinal propulsive activity, increases intestinal transit time, increases water/electrolyte absorption. Very limited CNS penetration at therapeutic doses, which is what differentiates it from centrally acting opioids.

## Evidence Summary
Strongly supported for reducing stool frequency and improving stool consistency at labeled OTC doses (Pharmacy Times 2026: Imodium is the 81% pharmacist-recommended antidiarrheal — a recommendation share, not a response rate; see Pharmacy Times Context below). The critical fact for this ingredient is the sharp split between therapeutic-dose safety and supratherapeutic/abuse-dose toxicity — these must be tracked as two separate facts, not blended.

## Pros
- Strong evidence for reducing stool frequency and improving stool consistency in acute diarrhea, measured by time to last unformed stool / number of unformed stools
- Loperamide + simethicone combination: clinical trials generally show faster relief of multiple acute-diarrhea symptoms than either ingredient alone (simethicone specifically addresses the bloating/gas component, not the diarrhea itself — don't attribute its effect to loperamide)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Treats the symptom, not the infectious cause
- Should not be casually used in: bloody diarrhea, high fever, suspected invasive bacterial dysentery, certain inflammatory diarrheas, or situations where inhibiting intestinal motility could worsen the underlying disease
- Constipation is a common expected effect

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Avoid in suspected invasive/inflammatory diarrhea (see above)

## Special Population Flags
- **Overdose/misuse (all populations):** massive supratherapeutic doses (far exceeding labeled OTC dosing) have produced serious cardiac toxicity: QT prolongation, torsades de pointes, ventricular arrhythmias, syncope, cardiac arrest. This is a misuse/abuse-dose phenomenon, not a therapeutic-dose risk — **do not infer that because therapeutic-dose loperamide is safe, high-dose loperamide is safe.** Store therapeutic-dose safety and supratherapeutic-dose toxicity as separate facts.

## Formulation Caveat
Loperamide alone (e.g. Imodium) is pharmacologically distinct from loperamide + simethicone combination products — the combination's broader symptom relief should not be read as evidence that simethicone treats diarrhea, or that plain loperamide provides the same gas/bloating relief.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Imodium — 81% of antidiarrheal category recommendations (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists). This means 81% of pharmacist recommendations in that category go to Imodium — it does not mean Imodium resolves diarrhea in 81% of patients.
