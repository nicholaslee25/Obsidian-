# Insect Bites (General Symptom Management)

**Type:** Care Protocol / Ingredient Class (not a single active ingredient)
**Category:** First Aid
**Evidence Grade:** Symptom management only — does not neutralize venom
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Itching, swelling, and erythema (redness) from insect bites.

## Evidence Summary
Typical treatments — hydrocortisone, antihistamines, calamine, and cold compresses — target symptom reduction (itching, swelling, erythema). Per source's explicit correction: "they do not 'neutralize' most insect venom after the fact." This distinguishes symptomatic relief from any claim of counteracting the bite/sting mechanism itself.

## Typical Treatments (per source)
- Hydrocortisone (see hydrocortisone.md)
- Antihistamines
- Calamine
- Cold compresses

## Pros
- Effective for reducing the symptoms of itching, swelling, and redness

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- None of the typical treatments neutralize insect venom after the fact — they address symptoms only, not the underlying bite/sting reaction mechanism

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Hydrocortisone-containing insect-bite products should be evaluated using hydrocortisone's own evidence page (hydrocortisone.md) — this page covers the general symptom-management framing across the whole treatment class.
