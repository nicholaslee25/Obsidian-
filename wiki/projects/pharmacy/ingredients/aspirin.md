# Aspirin

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** NSAID / irreversible platelet inhibitor
**Evidence Grade:** Strong for analgesia/antipyretic/anti-inflammatory use; low-dose cardiovascular-prevention benefit is now understood to apply to a substantially narrower population than once assumed
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Pain, fever, inflammation; low-dose use for cardiovascular event prevention in select patients; combination use for acute migraine (see Excedrin Migraine below).

## Mechanism
Unusually multi-purpose: analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory action, plus irreversible platelet inhibition (the basis for its cardiovascular use).

## Evidence Summary
Aspirin is described in the continuation source as "unusually multi-purpose." Low-dose aspirin has cardiovascular applications, but should not be casually treated as a general OTC preventive medication — modern primary-prevention evidence has substantially narrowed the group of people for whom benefit outweighs bleeding risk. Aspirin also appears in a combination product, Excedrin Migraine (acetaminophen + aspirin + caffeine), which can provide superior acute migraine relief versus placebo and versus some individual components, via a mechanistic rationale of acetaminophen (analgesia) + aspirin (analgesia/anti-inflammatory) + caffeine (analgesic adjuvant, possibly enhancing absorption/effect). A drawback specifically noted for this combination: frequent use of acute migraine medication can contribute to medication-overuse headache.

## Pros
- Multi-purpose: analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory, and antiplatelet effects in one molecule
- Excedrin Migraine combination can provide superior acute migraine relief vs. placebo and vs. some individual components

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Low-dose cardiovascular-prevention benefit applies to a substantially narrower population than historically assumed — modern primary-prevention evidence has narrowed the risk/benefit balance
- Bleeding risk from irreversible platelet inhibition
- Frequent use of acute migraine combination products (e.g., Excedrin Migraine) can contribute to medication-overuse headache
- General NSAID risk classes also apply (GI, renal, cardiovascular) — see ibuprofen.md/naproxen.md for the fuller NSAID safety discussion
- **Pediatric/adolescent viral illness: salicylate-containing products (aspirin) should not be casually used due to Reye syndrome risk** — this pediatric flag is explicitly discussed on the bismuth-subsalicylate.md page and applies to aspirin as the prototypical salicylate

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Anticoagulant/bleeding-risk situations
- Children/adolescents with viral illness (Reye syndrome risk)

## Special Population Flags
- Low-dose cardiovascular prevention: appropriate population is narrower than once assumed — this should not be treated as a general "everyone over a certain age should take it" recommendation
- Pediatric/adolescent viral illness: avoid (Reye syndrome risk)

## Formulation Caveat
Excedrin Migraine's evidence is for the acetaminophen + aspirin + caffeine combination specifically — do not extrapolate its migraine-specific efficacy data to plain aspirin used alone for a different indication, or vice versa.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Per the continuation source: Excedrin Migraine — 65% recommendation in its category (not a 65% migraine cure rate). This is a pharmacist recommendation share within the migraine-products category, not a response rate.
