## Evidence Summary
Even Pharmacy Times' own 2026 coverage emphasizes matching strain/dose/shelf-life potency rather than treating "probiotics" as one intervention. A proper evidence record needs: genus, species, strain, CFU (colony-forming-unit) dose, product, indication, population, duration, endpoint, comparator, storage conditions, and viability at administration. The source is explicit that "Lactobacillus" alone is not a sufficient identifier — evidence for one strain does not transfer to another strain of the same genus. This exact point is repeated in the source's "Important Negative Evidence" table: "Generic 'probiotics' | Cannot assume efficacy across strains."

## Pros
- Not reported in source material as a general class claim — any positive evidence would need to be strain-specific (none is given in source)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Cannot assume efficacy across strains — evidence for one strain does not transfer even to another strain of the same genus/species
- Viability at administration (i.e., whether the organisms are actually alive when taken) is a tracked variable, implying real-world potency/shelf-life failures are a known concern

## Formulation Caveat
Per the supplement database rule established in this source, "probiotics ≠ all probiotics" — genus, species, strain, and CFU dose must all be specified before any efficacy claim can be evaluated. A product record naming only "Lactobacillus" without a specific strain designation is not a sufficient identifier for evidence purposes.
