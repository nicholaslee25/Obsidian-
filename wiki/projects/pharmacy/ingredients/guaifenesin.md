# Guaifenesin

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Expectorant
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — human RCT evidence for pharmacologic effects on cough/respiratory symptoms, but effects on sputum volume/properties are inconsistent and benefits are generally modest
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md

---

## Indication
Productive/chesty cough and chest congestion associated with acute upper respiratory tract infection (URI).

## Mechanism
Intended to increase respiratory-tract fluid/secretions and facilitate mucus clearance ("expectorant" action). The mechanistic rationale is more consistently supported than the downstream clinical outcome (see Hoffer-Schaefer 2013 below).

## Evidence Summary
Human trials show a real pharmacologic effect on cough-reflex sensitivity and some subjective respiratory symptoms, and there's direct trial data on a commercial 1200 mg Mucinex dose specifically (not just generic guaifenesin). But the marketing claim that guaifenesin reliably "clears mucus" is not well supported — a well-controlled 2013 trial found no effect on actual sputum volume or properties. Overall: modest, inconsistent benefit, not a strong single effect.

### Studies

**Dicpinigaitis et al. — cough reflex sensitivity (acute viral URI)**
- PMID: 14665498
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- Population: 14 subjects with acute viral URI + 14 healthy volunteers
- Intervention: Guaifenesin 400 mg single dose
- Primary endpoint: Capsaicin cough-reflex sensitivity
- Result: Among URI subjects, mean log C5 after guaifenesin 0.92 ± 0.17 vs. placebo 0.66 ± 0.14 (P=0.028). No effect in healthy volunteers.
- Interpretation: Supports a real pharmacologic effect on cough-reflex sensitivity during URI — does not by itself prove meaningful overall symptom improvement.

**Albrecht et al. — extended-release guaifenesin, patient-reported outcomes**
- PMID: 23270519, DOI: 10.1186/1465-9921-13-118
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot/validation study, 7-day treatment
- Population: Adults with acute respiratory-tract infection symptoms; guaifenesin n=188, placebo n=190
- Dose: Extended-release guaifenesin 1200 mg every 12 hours
- Purpose: Evaluate patient-reported symptom improvement and validate a symptom-assessment instrument (not a definitive efficacy trial by itself).

**Hoffer-Schaefer et al., 2013 — sputum volume/properties (negative result)**
- PMID: 24003241, DOI: 10.4187/respcare.02640
- Design: Multicenter clinical trial, 8 days
- Population: 378 adolescents/adults with productive cough from acute RTI; guaifenesin n=188, placebo n=190
- Dose: Two 600 mg extended-release tablets twice daily for one week
- Result: Did **not** demonstrate an effect on sputum volume or sputum properties.
- Interpretation: Important negative mechanistic trial — "expectorant" marketing language should not be read as proof that guaifenesin measurably increases sputum clearance.

**Mucinex 1200 mg, single dose — acute RTI (brand-specific, not generic guaifenesin)**
- PMID: 26462765
- Design: Randomized, parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- Population: Non-smoking adults with acute upper RTI; Mucinex 1200 mg n=19, placebo n=19
- Dose: Two 600 mg extended-release guaifenesin tablets
- Measured: Mucociliary clearance, cough clearance, sputum rheology, surface/interfacial properties, subjective symptoms
- Interpretation: Direct evidence on a commercial Mucinex formulation rather than generic guaifenesin — should be read separately from trials using syrup or other formulations.

## Pros
- Real, measurable pharmacologic effect on cough-reflex sensitivity during acute URI
- Direct trial data exists on the actual commercial Mucinex 1200 mg formulation, not just the raw ingredient

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Effect on sputum volume/properties is inconsistent — a well-controlled 2013 trial found no effect at all
- Overall symptom benefit is modest and not "spectacular"
- GI upset, nausea, dizziness can occur

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material — no specific contraindications flagged beyond general GI tolerability

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material for guaifenesin specifically

## Formulation Caveat
Evidence applies to guaifenesin itself, not automatically to multi-ingredient combination products (e.g. Mucinex DM adds dextromethorphan, Mucinex D adds pseudoephedrine — those combinations should be evaluated as their own products, not assumed to inherit guaifenesin's evidence profile alone).
