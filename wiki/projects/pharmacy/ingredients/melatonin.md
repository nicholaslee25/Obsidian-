# Melatonin

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Hormone supplement
**Evidence Grade:** Age/indication-dependent — modest improvements in selected populations; not equivalent to a strong sedative
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Sleep-onset problems / circadian-rhythm disorders.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material beyond its classification as a hormone supplement.

## Evidence Summary
Meta-analyses show modest improvements in sleep-onset latency and circadian timing in selected populations; the effect is not equivalent to a strong sedative (dossier). A 2024 dose/timing review of 26 RCTs (1,689 observations) modeled increasing effects up to approximately 4 mg/day, with timing about 3 hours before desired bedtime modeled as potentially useful — but this does not establish 4 mg as the correct dose for everyone. A separate systematic review/meta-analysis of 24 RCTs in adults with non-comorbid chronic insomnia did NOT consistently demonstrate statistically significant improvement in sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, or sleep efficiency; effects were more apparent in pediatric/adolescent populations and in some comorbid-insomnia populations. Per source, the correct framing is "melatonin — age/indication dependent."

### Studies

**2024 dose/timing review**
- PMID: 38888087
- Design: Review, 26 RCTs, 1,689 observations
- Result: Dose-response modeling suggested increasing effects up to ~4 mg/day; timing ~3 hours before desired bedtime modeled as potentially useful.
- Interpretation: Does not establish 4 mg as correct for everyone.

**Chronic insomnia systematic review/meta-analysis**
- Design: Systematic review/meta-analysis of 24 RCTs
- Population: Adults with non-comorbid chronic insomnia
- Result: Statistically significant improvement NOT consistently demonstrated for sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, or sleep efficiency.
- Interpretation: Effects more apparent in pediatric/adolescent and some comorbid-insomnia populations. → "melatonin — age/indication dependent."
- Note: No PMID/DOI given in source material for this specific review.

## Pros
- Modest, real dose-response signal in the 2024 review (26 RCTs), with a plausible optimal timing window (~3 hours before bedtime)
- More apparent benefit in pediatric/adolescent and some comorbid-insomnia populations

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- In adults with non-comorbid chronic insomnia, benefit is NOT consistently demonstrated across a 24-RCT meta-analysis
- Daytime sleepiness, vivid dreams, and variable supplement quality (dossier)
- Do not equate OTC supplement evidence with FDA-approved drug evidence (dossier's explicit caution)

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Pediatric/adolescent populations show more apparent benefit than non-comorbid adult chronic insomnia
- Populations with comorbid insomnia (vs. non-comorbid) also show more apparent benefit in some studies

## Formulation Caveat
Per the supplement database rule established in this source, "melatonin tablet ≠ every extended-release formulation" — immediate-release and extended-release melatonin products should not be assumed to carry identical evidence.
