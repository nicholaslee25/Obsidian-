# Omega-3 / Fish Oil

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Dietary supplement (EPA/DHA)
**Evidence Grade:** Mixed — surrogate lipid improvement does not automatically imply cardiovascular-event reduction
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23

## Indication
Marketed for triglycerides, cardiovascular risk, inflammation, pregnancy/neurodevelopment support, and cognition.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material.

## Evidence Summary
The source insists on distinguishing EPA/DHA dietary supplements, prescription purified EPA, and dietary fish consumption — these are not interchangeable. Relevant endpoints include triglycerides, cardiovascular events, sudden cardiac death, stroke, inflammation, pregnancy/neurodevelopment, and cognition. A large triglyceride reduction does not automatically mean fewer heart attacks — this is called out explicitly as a "classic surrogate-endpoint trap." This exact point is repeated in the continuation source's "Important Negative Evidence" table: "Fish oil supplements | Triglyceride lowering ≠ automatically cardiovascular-event reduction."

## Pros
- Demonstrated ability to lower triglycerides (a surrogate marker)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Triglyceride lowering does not automatically translate into fewer cardiovascular events — explicitly flagged as a classic surrogate-endpoint trap and listed in the source's negative-evidence table
- EPA/DHA supplements, prescription purified EPA, and dietary fish consumption are not interchangeable and should not be evidence-pooled together

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Pregnancy/neurodevelopment is listed as a separate relevant endpoint, distinct from the cardiovascular questions

## Formulation Caveat
Per the supplement database rule established in this source, "fish oil ≠ all omega-3 products" — over-the-counter fish oil, prescription purified EPA, and dietary fish intake must be tracked as separate evidence categories.
