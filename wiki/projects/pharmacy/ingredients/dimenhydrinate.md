# Dimenhydrinate

**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — can reduce motion-sickness nausea/vomiting; dose must be age-specific

## Indication
Children's motion sickness (nausea/vomiting).

## Mechanism
Antihistamine with anticholinergic properties.

## Evidence Summary
Can reduce nausea/vomiting/motion-sickness symptoms in children. Adverse effects include sedation, dry mouth, blurred vision, and — notably — paradoxical excitation in some children (i.e., the opposite of sedation). Dose must be age-specific.

## Pros
- Can reduce nausea/vomiting/motion-sickness symptoms

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Sedation
- Dry mouth
- Blurred vision
- Paradoxical excitation in some children — an important counseling point since parents may expect only sedation and be caught off guard by the opposite reaction
- Dose must be age-specific — not a one-size dose across pediatric ages

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the general anticholinergic effect profile

## Special Population Flags
- **Children: dose must be age-specific; paradoxical excitation is a specifically documented pediatric risk with this ingredient**

## Formulation Caveat
