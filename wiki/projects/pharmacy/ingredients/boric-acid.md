## Evidence Summary
Boric acid is sometimes used for recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis, particularly recurrent/non-albicans Candida infections — a potential benefit, but not a universally appropriate treatment. Per source, it is toxic if swallowed, and there is a specific pregnancy safety concern. Formulation and patient population matter enormously for this ingredient — it is explicitly framed as a targeted-use product, not a general-purpose yeast-infection treatment (contrast with clotrimazole/miconazole, which are used for uncomplicated cases — see clotrimazole.md).

## Pros
- Potential benefit specifically for recurrent or non-albicans Candida infections, a population that may not respond as well to standard azole antifungals

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- **Toxic if swallowed**
- Not universally appropriate — formulation and patient population matter enormously
- **Pregnancy safety concern** — specifically flagged in source

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Pregnancy (specific safety concern per source)
- Oral ingestion — toxic if swallowed (this is a vaginal-use-only product)

## Special Population Flags
- **Pregnancy: specific safety concern flagged in source — do not treat as routinely safe in pregnancy**

## Formulation Caveat
Formulation and patient population matter enormously for boric acid specifically — it should be reserved for the recurrent/non-albicans Candida population per source's framing, not used as a general first-line yeast-infection treatment the way clotrimazole/miconazole are.
