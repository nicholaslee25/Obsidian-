## Indication
Multiple, separately evaluated indications: deficiency correction, migraine, constipation, pregnancy-related conditions, blood pressure, sleep, muscle cramps.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material.

## Evidence Summary
Magnesium is explicitly flagged as "highly indication-specific" in the source — deficiency, migraine, constipation, pregnancy-related conditions, blood pressure, sleep, and muscle cramps are separate evidence questions. The source gives a specific worked example of why this matters: "magnesium-as-laxative ≠ magnesium-for-deficiency ≠ proof it improves sleep." No individual RCT/study data is given for any of these indications in source material — this is a methodological framing note, not itemized study data.

## Pros
- Not reported in source material beyond the general indication list above (no specific positive-effect-size data given)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- The laxative effect of magnesium (e.g., magnesium hydroxide/citrate-type products) is a distinct pharmacologic use from magnesium-deficiency correction and should not be used as evidence for other claimed benefits (sleep, migraine, etc.)

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Pregnancy-related magnesium use is called out as its own separate evidence question, distinct from general deficiency correction or laxative use

## Formulation Caveat
Per the supplement database rule established in this source, magnesium oxide is explicitly distinguished from magnesium glycinate as an example of "never generalize evidence from an ingredient to every formulation containing it" — different magnesium salts should not be assumed interchangeable.
