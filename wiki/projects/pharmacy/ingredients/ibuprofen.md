# Ibuprofen

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** NSAID
**Evidence Grade:** High — strong RCT evidence for acute pain, fever, and inflammation
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Pain, fever, inflammation, dysmenorrhea (menstrual pain), musculoskeletal pain.

## Mechanism
COX inhibition, reducing prostaglandin synthesis.

## Evidence Summary
Strong randomized evidence for acute pain and fever; generally effective at OTC doses (dossier). The continuation source adds: versus acetaminophen, ibuprofen can be similar or superior for some acute pain, and inflammatory conditions generally favor NSAIDs since acetaminophen has little peripheral anti-inflammatory action. For menstrual pain specifically, NSAIDs (ibuprofen and naproxen) are among the best-supported OTC treatments for primary dysmenorrhea, working via reduced prostaglandin synthesis — directly targeting an important pathophysiologic mechanism — and outperform placebo for pain reduction and functional improvement; they are generally considered first-line pharmacologic therapy for this indication. Combination cold products containing ibuprofen add decongestant risks and duplication issues on top of ibuprofen's own risk profile (dossier).

## Pros
- Strong RCT evidence for acute pain and fever at OTC doses
- Genuine anti-inflammatory action (unlike acetaminophen) — can be similar or superior to acetaminophen for some acute pain and is generally preferred for inflammatory conditions
- Among the best-supported OTC treatments for primary dysmenorrhea (menstrual pain), considered first-line pharmacologic therapy for that indication

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- GI irritation/bleeding, renal risk, fluid retention, and cardiovascular considerations — general NSAID risk classes
- Risks rise with age, kidney disease, anticoagulant use, corticosteroid use, dehydration, and prolonged use
- Bronchospasm possible in susceptible patients
- Combination cold products add decongestant risks and duplication issues

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Anticoagulants, corticosteroids (increased risk when combined)
- NSAID contraindications generally apply (renal disease, dehydration, certain cardiovascular conditions)

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric ibuprofen (per continuation source):** effective for fever/pain, often allowed from 6 months of age depending on labeling. Avoid/use with caution in dehydration, renal disease, and certain GI conditions.

## Formulation Caveat
Combination cold products add decongestant risks and duplication issues on top of ibuprofen's own NSAID risk profile — do not evaluate the combination product's safety using ibuprofen's profile alone (dossier's explicit caveat).
