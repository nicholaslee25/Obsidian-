# Topical Anesthetics (Benzocaine / Lidocaine)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Local anesthetic
**Evidence Grade:** Established for temporary local analgesia; benzocaine carries a specific, important safety flag (methemoglobinemia)
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Minor pain, itching, oral lesions (including canker sores), superficial skin discomfort.

## Mechanism
Local anesthetic action (numbing).

## Evidence Summary
Used for minor pain, itching, oral lesions, and superficial skin discomfort. Benzocaine carries a rare but important risk of methemoglobinemia, particularly with excessive use and in infants/young children — per source, it is "not a benign 'numbing cream' in all populations." For canker sores specifically, benzocaine provides temporary local analgesia but does not necessarily accelerate healing or eliminate the underlying cause — symptom relief is not disease modification.

## Pros
- Effective temporary local analgesia for minor pain, itching, oral lesions, and superficial skin discomfort
- Rapid, direct symptomatic relief for canker-sore pain

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- **Benzocaine: rare but important risk of methemoglobinemia, particularly with excessive use and in infants/young children — not a benign product in all populations**
- For canker sores: symptom relief does not necessarily accelerate healing or eliminate the underlying cause

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Benzocaine: caution in infants/young children (methemoglobinemia risk)

## Special Population Flags
- **Infants/young children: benzocaine methemoglobinemia risk is specifically elevated in this population**

## Formulation Caveat
Benzocaine and lidocaine are treated together as a class in source material without a head-to-head efficacy or safety comparison — the methemoglobinemia risk described is specific to benzocaine, not established for lidocaine in source material.
