## What This Page Is
Glucose meters are evaluated against accuracy standards, not drug-style response rates. Per source: "accuracy is not one number."

## What to Measure and Why
**Relevant standards:** ISO 15197, FDA expectations, lab-reference comparison.

**Outcomes to track (per source):**
- Mean absolute relative difference (MARD)
- Percent of readings within ±15 mg/dL (for glucose <100 mg/dL) or ±15% (for glucose ≥100 mg/dL)
- Error-grid analysis
- Clinically significant error rate
- Strip lot variation
- Hematocrit effects
- Interfering substances — acetaminophen and vitamin C are specifically named as interferents in the source
- Temperature/humidity effects
- Expired strips
- Insufficient sample volume

## Why This Matters
No product-specific numerical accuracy data (MARD, % within ISO limits, etc.) for any named glucose meter is given in either source document. This page holds the evidence-schema framework the source specifies for future product-specific additions.

## Special Notes
- The interaction between glucose-meter accuracy and common OTC substances (acetaminophen, vitamin C) is explicitly flagged — a patient taking either could see a spuriously altered reading depending on meter chemistry.
- Strip lot variation and expired strips are named as real-world accuracy failure modes, separate from the meter hardware itself.
