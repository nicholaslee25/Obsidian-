# Simethicone

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antiflatulent (anti-gas)
**Evidence Grade:** Low — plausible mechanism, but clinical evidence for major symptomatic benefit is less impressive than consumer familiarity suggests; RCT evidence in infants specifically fails to show meaningful benefit
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Gas, bloating, pressure (general adult use); also widely used for infant colic/gas.

## Mechanism
Reduces surface tension of gas bubbles, facilitating their coalescence and passage.

## Evidence Summary
The mechanism is plausible, but clinical evidence for major symptomatic benefit is less impressive than consumer familiarity with the product would suggest — plausible mechanism does not equal strongly proven clinical benefit (continuation source). This gap is especially pronounced in infants: simethicone is widely used for infant colic/gas, but randomized evidence is not impressive, and several trials fail to show meaningful superiority over placebo. The source explicitly flags this as a "high-use, weak-evidence category" that "should be prominently encoded as such," and it also appears in the source's "Important Negative Evidence" table: "Infant simethicone | RCT evidence generally fails to show meaningful benefit."

## Pros
- Plausible, well-understood physical mechanism (reduces gas-bubble surface tension)
- Loperamide + simethicone combination products: clinical trials generally show faster relief of multiple acute-diarrhea symptoms than either alone, with simethicone specifically addressing the bloating/gas component (not the diarrhea itself — see loperamide.md, do not attribute this effect to loperamide)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Clinical evidence for major symptomatic benefit in general adult gas/bloating use is less impressive than consumer familiarity suggests
- **Infant gas/colic: randomized evidence is not impressive; several trials fail to show meaningful superiority over placebo.** This is a high-use, weak-evidence category and should be treated with the same caution as docusate's negative-evidence framing

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- **Infants:** widely used for colic/gas, but RCT evidence generally fails to show meaningful benefit over placebo — do not encode infant simethicone as a proven-effective colic treatment

## Formulation Caveat
Loperamide + simethicone combination products should not be read as evidence that plain loperamide treats gas/bloating, nor that plain simethicone treats diarrhea — each ingredient's contribution is distinct (see loperamide.md's formulation caveat).
