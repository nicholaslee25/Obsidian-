# Dextromethorphan

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antitussive (centrally acting; sigma-1/NMDA-related pharmacology)
**Evidence Grade:** Low/inconsistent — modest effect in adults, considerably weaker evidence in children
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Nonproductive (dry) acute cough.

## Mechanism
Centrally acting; sigma-1/NMDA-related pharmacology (per continuation source). A cough suppressant treats the symptom, not the underlying infection.

## Evidence Summary
Trials and systematic reviews show modest/inconsistent suppression of acute cough in adults; benefit varies by cause and population. Evidence is considerably weaker in children. Neither source provides individual RCT data for dextromethorphan specifically (unlike guaifenesin, pseudoephedrine, or naproxen) — the evidence here is class-level narrative. Dextromethorphan is also the single largest focus of a systematic review on OTC medicine misuse: 54 of 92 reviewed articles concerned DXM specifically.

## Pros
- Some reduction in cough vs. placebo demonstrated in adult studies

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Effect is modest and inconsistent; considerably weaker evidence in children
- Drowsiness, nausea, and drug interactions
- Serotonergic toxicity risk with some medicines (per continuation source: potential interaction with MAOIs, serotonergic antidepressants, other serotonergic drugs)
- Abuse/misuse risk at high doses; large doses can cause dissociation, hallucinations, agitation, and toxicity
- **OTC misuse systematic review (PMID 34025478):** 92 articles reviewed, focused on diphenhydramine, other first-generation antihistamines, dextromethorphan, codeine-containing cough medicines, and pseudoephedrine. Dextromethorphan was the largest single focus — 54 of 92 included articles concerned DXM. The review reported dose, route, toxicity, physical symptoms, psychiatric symptoms, and outcomes. Interpretation: this evidence base is important because OTC availability does not eliminate toxicity or misuse risk.

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Serotonergic drugs (MAOIs, serotonergic antidepressants, other serotonergic agents) — risk of serotonergic toxicity

## Special Population Flags
- Children: evidence for efficacy is considerably weaker than in adults; per the continuation source's general pediatric cough/cold section, adult formulations should not simply be dose-reduced and given to children, and cough/cold products carry meaningful pediatric safety concerns (dosing errors, sedation, agitation, tachycardia, accidental overdose)

## Formulation Caveat
A cough suppressant treats the symptom, not the underlying infection. Combination products containing dextromethorphan (e.g., paired with guaifenesin) should be evaluated as their own products — see guaifenesin.md's formulation caveat for the reciprocal point.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Cough Suppressants (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Delsym — 54%, Mucinex DM — 23%, Robitussin — 15%, Safe Tussin DM — 4%, NyQuil — 3%, Tukol Cough & Cold — 1%.
These are pharmacist recommendation shares within the Cough Suppressants category, not response rates. Note the "DM" naming convention in Mucinex DM and Safe Tussin DM conventionally denotes dextromethorphan content, consistent with the guaifenesin.md page's existing statement that "Mucinex DM (also contains dextromethorphan)."
