# Proton Pump Inhibitors (Omeprazole / Esomeprazole / Lansoprazole)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Proton pump inhibitor
**Evidence Grade:** Strong for acid suppression and healing of erosive esophagitis; long-term-use safety signals are observational and confounded, not established causal harms
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Frequent heartburn/GERD symptoms (OTC indication is specifically short-term treatment of frequent heartburn, not indefinite daily use).

## Mechanism
Irreversibly inhibits the gastric H+/K+-ATPase, producing much stronger acid suppression than antacids or H2 blockers.

## Evidence Summary
Strong randomized evidence for acid suppression and healing of erosive esophagitis; the OTC indication is short-term frequent heartburn, and OTC studies generally evaluate defined courses, not indefinite daily use (dossier). The continuation source elaborates: PPIs are among the best-supported classes for erosive esophagitis/GERD, generally outperforming H2 blockers for healing. They are not designed for "take one whenever you feel heartburn" use — for intermittent symptoms, antacids or H2 blockers may fit better, while PPIs work best used per their pharmacology (before meals, consistently). Long-term safety is controversial: observational studies associate prolonged use with kidney disease, fractures, C. diff infection, pneumonia, micronutrient deficiencies, and dementia — but association is not causation, confounding by indication is a major problem in this literature, and randomized evidence is generally more reassuring than the worst observational associations. The source explicitly instructs: do not encode "PPIs cause dementia" — the correct framing keeps the uncertainty explicit.

## Pros
- Strong randomized evidence for acid suppression and healing of erosive esophagitis
- Generally outperform H2 blockers for healing erosive esophagitis specifically
- Randomized evidence on long-term safety is generally more reassuring than the worst observational associations

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Headache/GI effects
- Not designed for as-needed intermittent use — works best used consistently before meals per its pharmacology
- Observational associations (not proven causal) with kidney disease, fractures, C. diff, pneumonia, micronutrient deficiencies, and dementia — confounding by indication is a major limitation of this evidence, and it should NOT be encoded as "PPIs cause dementia" or similar causal claims

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the long-term-use monitoring considerations above

## Special Population Flags
- Long-term use carries important monitoring/interaction considerations (dossier), though specific populations are not itemized in source

## Formulation Caveat
OTC studies generally evaluate defined treatment courses, not indefinite daily use — do not extrapolate short-course trial evidence to long-term daily OTC self-treatment without noting this gap.
