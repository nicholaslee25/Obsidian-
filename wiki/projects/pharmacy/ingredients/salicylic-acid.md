# Salicylic Acid

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Keratolytic
**Evidence Grade:** Evidence varies significantly by indication — better supported for wart removal than for acne
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Acne, hyperkeratosis, warts, corns/calluses.

## Mechanism
Keratolytic action (breaks down the outer skin layer).

## Evidence Summary
Evidence supports topical keratolysis; acne benefit is generally less robust than benzoyl peroxide/retinoids (dossier). The continuation source adds indication-specific nuance: for wart treatment specifically, salicylic acid is generally more effective than placebo/no treatment for common warts, but treatment can take weeks and adherence matters. Cryotherapy also works for warts but is associated with pain, blistering, and cost — presented as an alternative with its own tradeoffs, not a strictly superior option.

## Pros
- Generally more effective than placebo/no treatment for common warts
- Established keratolytic action supports multiple indications (acne, hyperkeratosis, corns/calluses)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Irritation; excessive application can cause systemic salicylate exposure in susceptible situations (dossier)
- For acne specifically, benefit is generally less robust than benzoyl peroxide or retinoids
- For warts, treatment can take weeks and requires sustained adherence

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the systemic-salicylate-exposure caution above

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Product concentration and body surface area matter (dossier) — evidence for one salicylic-acid concentration/indication (e.g., wart treatment) should not be assumed to transfer to another (e.g., acne).
