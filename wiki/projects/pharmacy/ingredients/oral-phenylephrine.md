# Oral Phenylephrine

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Sympathomimetic (oral) decongestant
**Evidence Grade:** No product-specific evidence supporting effectiveness — FDA concluded available data do NOT support oral phenylephrine as effective at recommended OTC doses. This is the flagship negative-evidence example in the source material.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Marketed for nasal congestion relief (the same indication as pseudoephedrine), historically sold as the go-to oral decongestant once pseudoephedrine sales were restricted behind the counter.

## Mechanism
Sympathomimetic alpha-adrenergic action, the same general mechanistic class as pseudoephedrine — but at current OTC oral doses, the mechanism does not translate into demonstrated clinical effectiveness (see below).

## Evidence Summary
This is explicitly called out in both source documents as the flagship negative-evidence example. High-quality clinical evidence has not established meaningful efficacy at current OTC oral doses. An FDA advisory review concluded the evidence did not support effectiveness, and the FDA proposed removing oral phenylephrine from the OTC monograph on lack-of-effectiveness grounds. Per the continuation source: "historically marketed as [a] nasal decongestant, but FDA concluded available data do NOT support oral phenylephrine as effective at recommended OTC doses... Textbook example of 'historical OTC status ≠ demonstrated efficacy' — do not teach that all decongestants work equally."

## Pros
- Not reported in source material — the source does not identify a demonstrated clinical benefit at OTC oral doses

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- FDA advisory review concluded available data do NOT support effectiveness at OTC doses; FDA proposed removing it from the OTC monograph on lack-of-effectiveness grounds
- Systemic sympathomimetic adverse effects remain possible even without demonstrated benefit
- Do not treat pharmacist recommendation share as proof of efficacy (per dossier source, explicitly flagged for this ingredient)

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
This evidence concerns oral phenylephrine specifically. It should not be extrapolated to topical/nasal-spray phenylephrine or confused with pseudoephedrine, which has substantially stronger evidence (see pseudoephedrine.md). The dossier explicitly distinguishes "Sudafed (pseudoephedrine)" from "Sudafed PE (phenylephrine)" as two separate products/ingredients in the same Decongestants, Oral category — do not conflate the two Sudafed product lines.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Decongestants, Oral (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Sudafed PE (phenylephrine) — 4%, vs. Sudafed (pseudoephedrine) — 45%. This is a recommendation share, not a response rate — and per the source's own framing, this ingredient in particular is the textbook case for why a recommendation percentage must never be read as proof of efficacy.
