# Antacids (Calcium Carbonate / Magnesium Hydroxide / Aluminum Hydroxide)

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** Antacids
**Evidence Grade:** Established for rapid symptomatic relief; specific salt determines the tradeoff profile (diarrhea vs. constipation)
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Heartburn, acid indigestion, upset stomach — rapid-onset symptomatic relief.

## Mechanism
Direct chemical neutralization of stomach acid (implied by class; not elaborated further in source material).

## Evidence Summary
Calcium carbonate (e.g., Tums) is inexpensive with rapid onset and high neutralizing capacity, but has downsides: constipation, belching, hypercalcemia with excess, and acid rebound. Magnesium salts are effective but carry a diarrhea/laxative effect. Aluminum salts are more likely to cause constipation. Combination magnesium+aluminum products are specifically noted as balancing magnesium's diarrhea tendency against aluminum's constipation tendency — described as "a useful pharmacologic principle" in source.

## Pros
- Rapid onset of symptomatic relief
- Calcium carbonate: inexpensive, high neutralizing capacity
- Mg+Al combination products can balance each salt's opposing GI side effects

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Calcium carbonate: constipation, belching, hypercalcemia with excess, acid rebound
- Magnesium salts: diarrhea/laxative effect
- Aluminum salts: more likely to cause constipation

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the GI side-effect profiles above

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Tums appearing in the Calcium Supplements Pharmacy Times category reflects its calcium carbonate content specifically, not an indication that antacid use is equivalent to osteoporosis therapy (per dossier source — see calcium.md).

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Antacids (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Tums — 52%, Gaviscon — 19%, Mylanta — 16%, Alka-Seltzer — 7%, Gelusil — 3%, Rolaids — 3%.
