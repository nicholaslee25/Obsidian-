# Naproxen

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** NSAID
**Evidence Grade:** High for acute moderate-to-severe pain (postoperative model); strong general NSAID evidence for musculoskeletal pain and dysmenorrhea
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

## Indication
Acute pain, inflammation, fever; acute musculoskeletal pain and dysmenorrhea; longer duration of action than ibuprofen.

## Mechanism
COX inhibition, reducing prostaglandin synthesis (general NSAID mechanism; not elaborated ingredient-specifically beyond this in source material).

## Evidence Summary
A Cochrane quantitative systematic review of single-dose naproxen/naproxen sodium for acute postoperative pain found strong, consistent efficacy at commonly used OTC doses, with a favorable number-needed-to-treat. Naproxen's longer duration of action versus ibuprofen is presented as both an advantage (longer relief) and a disadvantage (more prolonged exposure to NSAID-related adverse effects). Research on naproxen does not by itself establish benefit for other ingredients in a combination product.

### Studies

**Cochrane review — single-dose naproxen for acute postoperative pain**
- PMID: 15495091
- Design: Systematic review of 10 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials
- Population: 996 adults with moderate-to-severe acute postoperative pain
- Naproxen sodium 550 mg (6 trials, 500 patients): outcome = at least 50% pain relief over 4–6 hours. Effect: RR 4.2 (95% CI 2.9–6.0), NNT 2.6 (95% CI 2.2–3.2). Time to rescue medication: naproxen sodium 550 mg 7.6 hours vs. placebo 2.6 hours.
- Other doses (3 trials): naproxen 400 mg / naproxen sodium 440 mg — RR for ≥50% pain relief 4.8 (95% CI 2.75–8.38). Two small studies suggested effectiveness of naproxen 200 mg / naproxen sodium 220 mg.
- Adverse events: No significant difference in the number experiencing any adverse event vs. placebo in the pooled trials; reporting was inconsistent.
- Interpretation: Naproxen is a well-supported acute analgesic; evidence is strongest for moderate-to-severe postoperative pain, not necessarily for every OTC indication it is used for.

## Pros
- Strong, consistent RCT evidence (Cochrane-level) for acute pain relief with a favorable NNT
- Longer duration of action than ibuprofen — fewer doses needed per day

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- GI bleeding, renal and cardiovascular risks — same major NSAID safety classes as ibuprofen
- Longer duration of action also means more prolonged exposure to NSAID-related adverse effects per dose
- Avoid duplicate NSAID use (e.g., taking naproxen and ibuprofen together)

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the general GI/renal/cardiovascular NSAID risk classes noted above

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material specifically for naproxen (general NSAID population cautions apply — see ibuprofen.md for the fuller pediatric/menstrual-pain NSAID discussion)

## Formulation Caveat
Research on naproxen does not establish benefit for the other ingredients in a combination product; use naproxen-specific evidence only for naproxen-specific claims.
