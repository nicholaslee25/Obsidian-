# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antioxidant/mitochondrial cofactor supplement
**Evidence Grade:** Antioxidant activity is mechanistic, not a demonstrated patient benefit — strongest open research questions remain unresolved in source material
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Marketed for cardiovascular support, energy, antioxidant effects, and statin-associated muscle symptoms.

## Mechanism
Antioxidant activity (mechanistic — not itself evidence of patient benefit, per source).

## Evidence Summary
The strongest research questions identified in source are heart failure, statin-associated muscle symptoms, migraine, and blood-pressure/metabolic endpoints. The source explicitly cautions that antioxidant activity is mechanistic, not a demonstrated patient benefit — i.e., showing CoQ10 has antioxidant properties in a lab sense does not by itself prove a clinical outcome improves.

## Pros
- Plausible mechanistic rationale (antioxidant/mitochondrial cofactor) for several of its marketed uses

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Antioxidant activity is mechanistic only — not established in source material as translating into a demonstrated patient-important benefit for any of its marketed indications (heart failure, statin-associated muscle symptoms, migraine, BP/metabolic endpoints)

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Per the supplement database rule established in this source, "CoQ10 ubiquinone ≠ every ubiquinol formulation" — different CoQ10 formulations should not be assumed interchangeable in evidence.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Nature Made leads CoQ10 recommendation at 51% (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists) — a recommendation share, not a clinical response rate.
