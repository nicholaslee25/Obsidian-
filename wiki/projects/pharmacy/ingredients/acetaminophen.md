# Acetaminophen

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Analgesic/antipyretic (central-acting; not a peripheral anti-inflammatory NSAID)
**Evidence Grade:** High for short-term analgesia/fever reduction generally; Low/mixed specifically for common-cold symptom relief
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Pain and fever generally; also marketed within cold/flu combination products for cold-associated symptoms (nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea, headache/aches).

## Mechanism
Predominantly a central analgesic/antipyretic; not a peripheral anti-inflammatory agent the way NSAIDs are.

## Evidence Summary
For general pain/fever, acetaminophen has strong supporting evidence, though efficacy is symptom-specific rather than antiviral. For common-cold symptoms specifically, a Cochrane review of 4 RCTs (758 participants) could not be pooled due to differences in design/outcomes/time points, and found inconsistent results: nasal obstruction improved significantly in 2 of 4 studies, one study found acetaminophen superior to placebo for rhinorrhea, and there was no convincing improvement for sore throat, malaise, sneezing, or cough. Headache/aches improved in 2 of 3 studies. No study reported an effect on common-cold duration. A separate review of OTC analgesics (aspirin, acetaminophen, ibuprofen) for colds/flu found supporting evidence for symptomatic relief generally, with a relatively small direct cold/flu evidence base, and found no evidence that these drugs prolong colds/flu by suppressing fever or immune response.

### Studies

**Cochrane review — acetaminophen (paracetamol) for the common cold in adults**
- PMID: 23818046
- Design: Systematic review, 4 RCTs, 758 participants; not pooled due to substantial differences in designs, outcomes, and time points
- Results: Nasal obstruction — significant improvement in 2/4 studies. Rhinorrhea — one study found acetaminophen superior to placebo. No convincing improvement for sore throat, malaise, sneezing, or cough. Headache/aches — 2 studies favored acetaminophen, 1 study found no difference. Duration — none of the included studies reported common-cold duration.
- Adverse effects: GI events, dizziness, dry mouth, somnolence, increased sweating.
- Interpretation (per source): Acetaminophen may relieve some cold symptoms, particularly nasal obstruction/rhinorrhea and headache/aches, but the evidence base is sparse and does not establish shortening of the illness.

**NSAIDs/acetaminophen review for cold and flu**
- PMID: 16882099
- Design: Narrative review of OTC analgesics for colds and influenza (covers aspirin, paracetamol/acetaminophen, and ibuprofen together)
- Finding: These drugs have evidence supporting symptomatic relief, but the direct cold/flu evidence base was relatively small. The review did not find evidence that these drugs prolong colds/flu by suppressing fever or immune responses.
- Interpretation: This review is class-level (covers three analgesics together), not acetaminophen-specific data alone.

## Pros
- Strong general evidence for short-term analgesia and fever reduction
- Lower GI toxicity than NSAIDs, no platelet inhibition (unlike aspirin) — preferable when NSAIDs are inappropriate
- Some evidence for relief of specific cold symptoms (nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea, headache/aches), though inconsistent across studies

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Important dose-dependent hepatotoxicity risk, especially with overdose or duplicate acetaminophen-containing combination products
- **Most important OTC safety issue (per continuation source): duplicate ingredient exposure** — acetaminophen appears across many combination products (e.g., a cold-formula tablet, a nighttime formula, and a fever product used together), and total daily acetaminophen exposure must be tracked across products, not per individual package
- No convincing evidence for sore throat, malaise, sneezing, or cough relief specifically; no evidence of shortened cold duration
- Adverse effects reported in the cold-symptom Cochrane review: GI events, dizziness, dry mouth, somnolence, increased sweating

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Hepatotoxicity risk compounds with any other acetaminophen-containing product taken concurrently (see duplicate-ingredient warning above)

## Special Population Flags
- **Pediatric (per continuation source):** effective antipyretic/analgesic, but dose must be weight-based. Major risk is concentration errors and duplicate dosing — historically, differing pediatric liquid concentrations caused significant dosing errors. Always verify weight, concentration, mL dose, dosing interval, and max daily dose.

## Formulation Caveat
Use research on acetaminophen for the symptom being treated; do not infer efficacy of an entire branded combination product from acetaminophen's evidence alone (per dossier source). The Pharmacy Times guide's own "Correction 2" section explicitly gives "Tylenol → acetaminophen" as an example of a brand-to-ingredient mapping, underscoring that brand recommendation data and ingredient efficacy data must be tracked separately.
