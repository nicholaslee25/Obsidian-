# Calcium

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Mineral supplement
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — supports correction of inadequate intake; fracture-prevention effects depend heavily on population, baseline intake, and vitamin D status
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Bone health / correcting inadequate calcium intake.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material.

## Evidence Summary
Evidence supports correction of inadequate calcium intake; fracture-prevention effects depend on population, baseline intake, and vitamin D status (dossier). The continuation source elaborates: there are two separate evidence questions — correcting inadequate intake vs. supplementing already-sufficient intake — with outcomes spanning bone mineral density, osteopenia, fractures, colorectal outcomes, cardiovascular outcomes, and kidney stones. A proper entry must specify baseline dietary intake, menopausal status, vitamin D status, dose, duration, fracture endpoint, and adverse events — not just "calcium strengthens bones."

## Pros
- Evidence supports correction of inadequate calcium intake

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Constipation, kidney-stone considerations in some populations
- Excessive intake is not automatically beneficial
- Fracture-prevention benefit is population-, dose-, and vitamin-D-status-dependent — not a universal effect

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material beyond the kidney-stone consideration above

## Special Population Flags
- Menopausal status and baseline vitamin D status affect the relevant evidence question and should be tracked separately (per continuation source)

## Formulation Caveat
Tums appearing in the calcium-supplements category reflects its calcium carbonate content, not an indication that antacid use is equivalent to osteoporosis therapy (dossier's explicit caveat).

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Calcium Supplements (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Citracal — 36%, Nature Made — 24%, Caltrate — 23%, Tums — 7%, Nature's Bounty — 6%, Viactiv — 2%, vitafusion — 2%.
