# Second-Generation Antihistamines

**Type:** Active Ingredient (class)
**Drug Class:** H1-receptor antagonists (examples: cetirizine, levocetirizine, loratadine, desloratadine, fexofenadine)
**Evidence Grade:** Strong for allergic rhinitis symptoms; between-drug differences are mostly small/clinically trivial per network meta-analysis
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Allergic rhinitis symptoms: sneezing, itching, rhinorrhea.

## Mechanism
All block H1 histamine receptors.

## Evidence Summary
Strong evidence for reducing sneezing, itching, and rhinorrhea in allergic rhinitis (dossier). A 2026 network meta-analysis of 74 RCTs for allergic rhinitis found between-drug differences were mostly small/clinically trivial/low-certainty — this prevents the false claim that any one second-generation antihistamine is "universally the best" (continuation source). These do not treat viral influenza itself. No individual study PMID/effect-size data is given in source beyond the network meta-analysis description.

### Studies

**2026 network meta-analysis of allergic rhinitis treatments**
- Design: Network meta-analysis, 74 RCTs
- Population: Allergic rhinitis
- Result: Between-drug differences among second-generation antihistamines were mostly small, clinically trivial, and low-certainty.
- Interpretation: Prevents the false claim that one second-generation antihistamine is universally superior to the others; individual sedation profile is a more useful differentiator than assumed efficacy differences (see below).
- Note: No PMID/DOI given in source material for this specific network meta-analysis.

## Pros
- Strong evidence for reducing sneezing, itching, and rhinorrhea in allergic rhinitis
- Generally less sedating than first-generation antihistamines, though individual sedation can occur

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Do not treat viral influenza itself
- Sedation tendency is individual-dependent: fexofenadine has very low CNS penetration; loratadine is generally low-sedation; cetirizine is more likely to cause drowsiness than some other second-generation agents; levocetirizine can also cause somnolence

## Contraindications / Interactions

## Special Population Flags

## Formulation Caveat
Combination-product evidence (e.g., "-D" decongestant combinations like Claritin-D, Zyrtec-D, Allegra-D) should be separated from single-agent second-generation-antihistamine evidence — see pseudoephedrine.md for the hedged brand notes on those combination products.

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Antihistamines, Oral (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Zyrtec — 34%, Claritin — 30%, Allegra — 17%, Benadryl — 8%, Xyzal — 6%, Alavert — 5%.
Note: Benadryl (diphenhydramine) is a first-generation antihistamine, not a second-generation one, despite appearing in the same Pharmacy Times category table — see the source's separate "First-generation antihistamines" ingredient entry.
