# Vitamin D

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Vitamin/hormone-precursor supplement
**Evidence Grade:** High for correcting deficiency; No product-specific evidence for disease-prevention benefit in already-replete people — this is a deliberately preserved negative-evidence framing
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

## Indication
Correction of vitamin D deficiency. Also widely marketed/used for broader disease prevention in people who are not deficient — a separate and much weaker evidence question.

## Mechanism
Not elaborated in source material.

## Evidence Summary
The source insists on distinguishing deficiency treatment from supplementation in already-replete people, since these are different evidence questions. Deficiency treatment is clearly important. High-dose supplementation in replete people does NOT establish prevention of cancer, cardiovascular disease, infection, depression, fractures generally, or mortality. A specific 2026 example is preserved deliberately as a negative study: the phase 3 SOLARIS trial tested high-dose vitamin D3 plus first-line chemotherapy in metastatic colorectal cancer — it was safe, but showed no significant progression-free survival (PFS) improvement. Per source: "Kept explicitly as a negative study so the model doesn't learn 'vitamin D is good for cancer.'"

### Studies

**SOLARIS trial, 2026**
- Design: Phase 3 trial, high-dose vitamin D3 + first-line chemotherapy vs. (implied) chemotherapy alone
- Population: Metastatic colorectal cancer
- Result: Safe, but no significant PFS (progression-free survival) improvement.
- Interpretation: Deliberately preserved negative study — high-dose vitamin D3 supplementation did not demonstrate a cancer-outcome benefit in this trial.
- Note: No PMID/DOI given in source material for this trial.

## Pros
- Clearly important and well-established for correcting diagnosed vitamin D deficiency

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- High-dose supplementation in already-replete people does NOT establish prevention of cancer, cardiovascular disease, infection, depression, fractures generally, or mortality
- SOLARIS trial (2026): safe, but no significant PFS improvement in metastatic colorectal cancer when added to chemotherapy

## Formulation Caveat
Always distinguish deficiency-correction evidence from disease-prevention evidence in replete individuals — these are separate evidence questions per the source's explicit methodological framing, and conflating them risks the database "learning" an unsupported claim like "vitamin D is good for cancer."
