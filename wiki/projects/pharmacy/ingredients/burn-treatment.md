## Indication
Minor thermal burns.

## Evidence Summary
Evidence-based first aid for minor thermal burns centers on a protocol, not a product: cooling with cool running water, removing constrictive objects (e.g., rings, tight clothing near the burn), protecting the wound, and avoiding ice directly on the tissue. Topical products are explicitly described as secondary to this protocol. The source specifically advises avoiding butter, grease, and other irritating household substances — a common folk remedy that is not supported.

## The Protocol (per source)
1. Cool the burn with cool running water
2. Remove constrictive objects near the burn site
3. Protect the wound
4. Avoid applying ice directly to the tissue
5. Avoid butter, grease, or other irritating household substances

## Pros
- Simple, low-cost, evidence-based protocol requiring no special product

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Ice applied directly to burned tissue is specifically discouraged (can worsen tissue injury)
- Butter, grease, and other household substances are explicitly discouraged despite being common folk remedies

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Topical burn products (ointments, gels, sprays) are secondary to the cooling/protection protocol above — the source does not identify a specific topical product with demonstrated superiority over the basic protocol.
