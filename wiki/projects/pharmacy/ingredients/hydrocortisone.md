# Hydrocortisone

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Topical corticosteroid (low-potency, 1% OTC strength)
**Evidence Grade:** High — among the best-established inflammatory-dermatosis treatments
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23

## Indication
Itch/inflammation from dermatitis, eczema, and insect-bite reactions.

## Mechanism
Topical corticosteroid anti-inflammatory action.

## Evidence Summary
Strong evidence for short-term relief of inflammatory/pruritic dermatoses (dossier). The continuation source elaborates: hydrocortisone 1% is a low-potency topical corticosteroid used for eczema, dermatitis, inflammatory itching, and insect-bite reactions, and is among the best-established inflammatory-dermatosis treatments. Important variables are potency, formulation, body site, duration, and frequency of use.

## Pros
- Among the best-established inflammatory-dermatosis treatments per source
- Effective for short-term relief of itch/inflammation from multiple causes (dermatitis, eczema, insect bites)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- With excessive/prolonged use: skin atrophy, telangiectasia, striae, steroid acne, hypopigmentation, and systemic absorption in unusual circumstances
- Thin-skin areas (face, genitals, skin folds) are more vulnerable to these adverse effects
- Topical steroid evidence does not support homeopathic topical analgesic claims (dossier's explicit caveat — do not conflate the two categories)

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Avoid inappropriate use on infection or on face/genitals without guidance (dossier)

## Special Population Flags
- Thin-skin body sites (face, genitals, folds) require extra caution regardless of age

## Formulation Caveat
Potency, formulation, body site, duration, and frequency all matter — a given hydrocortisone product's evidence should not be assumed to transfer identically across all of these variables.
