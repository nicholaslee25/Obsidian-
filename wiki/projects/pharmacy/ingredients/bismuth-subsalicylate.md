# Bismuth Subsalicylate

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Antidiarrheal/antacid (also has antimicrobial activity)
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — clinical trials support symptomatic treatment of acute diarrhea and dyspepsia
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Diarrhea, upset stomach, heartburn; also used in some infectious-diarrhea contexts.

## Mechanism
Antisecretory effects, antimicrobial activity against some organisms, and salicylate-mediated effects.

## Evidence Summary
Clinical trials support symptomatic treatment of acute diarrhea and dyspepsia; the ingredient also has antimicrobial activity against some organisms (dossier). The continuation source elaborates on adverse effects and specific precautions: black tongue/stool is caused by bismuth sulfide formation (a benign but alarming-looking effect); contraindications/cautions include salicylate allergy and certain anticoagulant/bleeding situations; and there is a specific, important pediatric warning.

## Pros
- Clinical trial support for symptomatic treatment of acute diarrhea and dyspepsia
- Antimicrobial activity against some organisms, distinct from loperamide's purely motility-based mechanism

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Black tongue/stool (bismuth sulfide) — a benign but alarming cosmetic effect worth counseling on
- Salicylate-related bleeding/interaction risk
- Tinnitus/salicylism with excess use
- **Pediatric warning: salicylate-containing products should not be casually used in children/adolescents with viral illness (Reye syndrome risk).**

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Salicylate allergy
- Certain anticoagulant/bleeding situations

## Special Population Flags
- **Children/adolescents with viral illness: avoid — Reye syndrome risk from the salicylate component.**

## Formulation Caveat
Pepto-Bismol/Kaopectate-type evidence should be separated from loperamide evidence — the two act by entirely different mechanisms despite both being marketed as antidiarrheals (dossier's explicit caveat; see loperamide.md).

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
Antidiarrheals (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Imodium — 81%, Pepto-Bismol — 10%, DiaResQ — 6%, Kaopectate — 3%.
