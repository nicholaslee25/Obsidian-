# Oscillococcinum

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Homeopathic preparation (highly diluted — explicitly a different evidence category from pharmacologically active herbal extracts like elderberry or Pelargonium)
**Evidence Grade:** Low/uncertain — weak/uncertain evidence; no defensible overall cure percentage
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md

---

## Indication
Marketed for influenza and influenza-like illness prevention and treatment.

## Mechanism
Not reported in source material (homeopathic preparation; the dossier explicitly flags homeopathic products as requiring separate treatment from pharmacologically active herbal extracts rather than assigning a pharmacologic mechanism).

## Evidence Summary
The most recent major Cochrane review (2015) pooled 6 studies (2 prevention studies, 327 participants; 4 treatment studies, 1,196 participants). For symptom relief at 48 hours the pooled result favored Oscillococcinum, but the effect shrank and lost robustness at later timepoints (3, 4, 5 days), and the review characterized the overall evidence as low quality/insufficient, not supporting first-line treatment. Prevention data did not reach statistical significance.

### Studies

**Cochrane review, 2015**
- PMID: 25629583
- Design: Systematic review, 6 studies (2 prevention studies, 327 participants; 4 treatment studies, 1,196 participants)
- Symptom relief at 48 hours: absolute risk difference 7.7 percentage points; RR 1.86 (95% CI 1.27–2.73), P=0.001
- At 3 days: RR 1.27 (95% CI 1.03–1.56)
- At 4 days: RR 1.11 (95% CI 0.98–1.27)
- At 5 days: RR 1.06 (95% CI 0.96–1.16)
- Prevention: RR 0.48 (95% CI 0.17–1.34), P=0.16
- Conclusion (per review): The evidence is low quality/insufficient and does not support first-line treatment.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25629583/ ; https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001957_homeopathic-oscillococcinumr-preventing-and-treating-influenza-and-influenza-illness

**Overall (per source):** Weak/uncertain evidence. No defensible overall cure percentage. The apparent 48-hour benefit weakens progressively at later timepoints (3, 4, 5 days), which is itself informative — a real, durable treatment effect would not be expected to fade this quickly toward the null.

## Pros
- The only one of the four homeopathic-flu products in this dossier with a dedicated Cochrane systematic review pooling multiple RCTs

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Effect size at 48 hours, while statistically significant, shrinks toward the null by day 4–5 (RR approaching 1.0)
- Prevention effect was not statistically significant (95% CI 0.17–1.34 crosses 1)
- Cochrane's own characterization: low quality/insufficient evidence, does not support first-line treatment

## Contraindications / Interactions

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Per the dossier's "Correction 4," Oscillococcinum (a highly diluted homeopathic preparation) must be evidence-graded separately from pharmacologically active herbal extracts such as Pelargonium/EPs 7630 or elderberry, even though all three appear together in "Homeopathic ___ Products" Pharmacy Times categories. Grouping them together would obscure that Oscillococcinum's evidence quality is categorically different (and weaker) than Pelargonium's.
