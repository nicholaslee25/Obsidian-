# Polyethylene Glycol 3350 (PEG 3350)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Osmotic laxative
**Evidence Grade:** High — among the strongest evidence bases of OTC constipation products
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Constipation.

## Mechanism
Osmotic action — retains water in the intestinal lumen.

## Evidence Summary
Strong evidence for increasing bowel movements and improving stool consistency in chronic constipation, with evidence stronger than for many stimulant/herbal laxatives (dossier). The continuation source calls PEG "among the strongest evidence bases of OTC constipation products," citing increased stool frequency, improved consistency, and reduced straining vs. placebo. A head-to-head RCT against tegaserod found a genuine patient-level treatment-success rate.

### Studies

**PEG vs. tegaserod RCT**
- Design: Randomized controlled trial, N=237
- Result: Treatment success 50.0% (PEG) vs. 30.8% (tegaserod), P=.003
- Interpretation: Per source, this is "a genuine patient-level success rate, not a recommendation percentage" — explicitly distinguished from Pharmacy Times survey data.
- Note: No PMID/DOI given in source material for this trial.

## Pros
- Among the strongest OTC constipation evidence bases per source
- Increased stool frequency, improved consistency, reduced straining vs. placebo
- Head-to-head RCT vs. tegaserod showed a genuine patient-level success-rate advantage (50.0% vs. 30.8%, P=.003)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Bloating, diarrhea, cramping
- Prolonged unexplained constipation needs medical evaluation rather than continued self-treatment
