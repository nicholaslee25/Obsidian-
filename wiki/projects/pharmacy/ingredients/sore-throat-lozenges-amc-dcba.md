# Sore Throat Lozenges — AMC/DCBA (Amylmetacresol + 2,4-Dichlorobenzyl Alcohol)

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** Local antiseptic/analgesic throat lozenge (e.g., Strepsils-type formulation)
**Evidence Grade:** Moderate — consistent effect across RCTs, but major limitation is that all included trials were manufacturer-sponsored
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Acute uncomplicated sore throat pain relief.

## Mechanism
Local symptomatic analgesia (distinguished in source from flurbiprofen lozenges, which act via local NSAID-mediated anti-inflammatory/analgesic action). Neither treats the underlying viral infection.

## Evidence Summary
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 3 RCTs (660 adults) found a significant reduction in pain intensity around 2 hours post-treatment (SMD −0.60, 95% CI −0.75 to −0.45) favoring the active lozenge, with no serious adverse events. A large independent RCT (N=310) found pain reduction detectable as early as 5 minutes, persisting about 2 hours, with improvements also in swallowing difficulty and functional impairment. A third RCT combining AMC/DCBA with lidocaine did not reach significance on its primary endpoint, though several secondary endpoints favored active treatment — kept in this record as an example of a partially negative trial. The major limitation across the pooled evidence is that all included trials were manufacturer-sponsored.

### Studies

**Weckmann G, Hauptmann-Voß A, Baumeister SE, Klötzer C, Chenot JF — systematic review and meta-analysis**
- Citation: Efficacy of AMC/DCBA lozenges for sore throat: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Int J Clin Pract. 2017. PMID: 28869700, DOI: 10.1111/ijcp.13002
- Design: 77 citations screened, 3 RCTs met inclusion criteria; N=660 adults with acute uncomplicated sore throat
- Formulation: Amylmetacresol 0.6 mg + 2,4-dichlorobenzyl alcohol 1.2 mg
- Primary endpoint: Pain intensity ~2 hours post-treatment
- Result: SMD −0.60 (95% CI −0.75 to −0.45) on an 11-point pain scale, favoring active lozenge
- Adverse events: No serious AEs; mild effects (headache, cough, nasal congestion, oral irritation) in up to ~16% of subjects in both groups
- Limitations: **All included trials were manufacturer-sponsored.**

**McNally D, et al. — large RCT**
- Citation: Rapid relief of acute sore throat with AMC/DCBA throat lozenges: RCT. PMID: 19849767, DOI: 10.1111/j.1742-1241.2009.02230.x
- Design: N=310 (155 active/155 placebo)
- Result: Pain reduction detectable at 5 minutes, persisting ~2 hours. Also improved sore-throat relief, swallowing difficulty, functional impairment. No AE difference between groups.

**AMC/DCBA + lidocaine RCT**
- PMID: 22579007
- Design: N=190, randomized to AMC/DCBA + lidocaine, hexylresorcinol, or placebo
- Result: AMC/DCBA + lidocaine onset ~1–10 min; hexylresorcinol onset ~1–5 min. AMC/DCBA + lidocaine did NOT reach significance on its primary endpoint, though several secondary endpoints favored active treatment.
- Interpretation: Kept as an example of a partially negative trial.

## Pros
- Consistent, statistically significant pain-relief signal across a meta-analysis and an independent large RCT
- Onset detectable within minutes in the large RCT (McNally et al.)
- No serious adverse events reported

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- All trials in the meta-analysis were manufacturer-sponsored — a major limitation on independence of the evidence
- The AMC/DCBA + lidocaine combination trial did not reach significance on its primary endpoint

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Not reported in source material

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material — studies were conducted in adults

## Formulation Caveat
AMC/DCBA (local antiseptic/analgesic action) is mechanistically distinct from flurbiprofen lozenges (local NSAID anti-inflammatory/analgesic action) — see sore-throat-lozenges-flurbiprofen.md. Neither treats the underlying viral infection.
