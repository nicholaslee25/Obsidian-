# Famotidine

**Type:** Active Ingredient
**Drug Class:** H2-receptor antagonist
**Evidence Grade:** Strong for acid suppression/heartburn relief; less potent than PPIs for healing erosive disease
**Last updated:** 2026-08-23
**Sources:** raw/pharmacy-research/otc-guide-master-dossier-volume-2.md, raw/pharmacy-research/otc-evidence-corpus-chatgpt-continuation.md

---

## Indication
Heartburn, acid indigestion, prevention of meal-associated heartburn, short-term acid symptoms.

## Mechanism
Blocks histamine H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells, decreasing acid secretion.

## Evidence Summary
Strong evidence for acid suppression and relief of frequent heartburn, though less potent than proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) for healing erosive disease (dossier). The continuation source adds: extensive RCT evidence exists for acid suppression/heartburn improvement, useful for intermittent symptoms needing relatively rapid suppression without continuous PPI therapy. PPI superiority for stronger/more sustained suppression does not make H2 blockers inferior for every patient — the right choice depends on symptom pattern. Tachyphylaxis (reduced acid-suppressive effect with repeated, especially continuous, exposure) is a specifically noted limitation and is one reason PPIs dominate for frequent GERD.

## Pros
- Strong RCT evidence for acid suppression and heartburn relief
- Useful for intermittent symptoms needing rapid-ish suppression without committing to continuous PPI therapy
- Current Zantac 360° products use famotidine (per dossier's "Correction 2" worked example: "Pepcid → famotidine")

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Less potent than PPIs for healing erosive esophagitis/GERD
- Tachyphylaxis: repeated exposure, especially continuous use, can reduce acid-suppressive effect
- Headache/GI effects
- Renal dosing considerations

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Renal impairment requires dosing consideration (per dossier)

## Special Population Flags
- Renal impairment: dosing considerations apply

## Formulation Caveat
Current Zantac 360° products use famotidine; older ranitidine-based Zantac evidence is NOT interchangeable with famotidine evidence — the source explicitly flags this brand-name continuity trap (same brand name, different active ingredient across product generations).

## Pharmacy Times Context (recommendation share — NOT efficacy)
H2 Receptor Antagonists (Pharmacy Times 2026 OTC Guide, survey of ~1,700 pharmacists): Pepcid — 83%, Tagamet HB 200 — 9%, Zantac 360° — 8%.
Per the continuation source: "Pepcid 83%" is explicitly named as a recommendation-share example, not a response rate.
