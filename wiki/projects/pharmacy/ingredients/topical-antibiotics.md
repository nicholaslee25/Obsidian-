## Indication
Minor cuts and scrapes.

## Mechanism
Antibiotic action against skin-surface bacteria (mechanism not elaborated further per individual agent in source material).

## Evidence Summary
The important point per source: topical antibiotics are not always superior to simple wound care with petrolatum — a clean, moist wound environment can heal effectively without antibiotics. Topical antibiotics can cause contact dermatitis, especially neomycin. The source's explicit correction: "'Antibiotic ointment = automatically better' is incorrect." This is also captured in the source's "Important Negative Evidence" table: "OTC topical antibiotic ointment | Not automatically superior to petrolatum for uncomplicated wounds."

## Pros
- Antibiotic action may be beneficial in some wound contexts (not further specified in source)

## Cons / Safety Limitations
- Not automatically superior to simple wound care with petrolatum for uncomplicated minor wounds
- Can cause contact dermatitis, especially with neomycin specifically

## Contraindications / Interactions
- Neomycin sensitivity/contact dermatitis risk

## Special Population Flags
- Not reported in source material

## Formulation Caveat
Bacitracin, polymyxin B, and neomycin (alone or in combination) are not necessarily interchangeable — neomycin in particular carries a specifically higher contact-dermatitis risk per source.
